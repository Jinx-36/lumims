# CONTENT.md — Lumims Curriculum

## 1. Content mission

Lumims teaches photography and video progressively, starting from zero knowledge and gradually moving toward confident Lumix GH5 configuration.

The curriculum should feel like W3Schools:

- clearly separated topics
- short lessons
- obvious next/previous navigation
- practical examples
- exercises
- reference material
- visible progress

Important lessons should connect theory to the camera using this learning sequence:

1. **Theory**
2. **GH5 Configuration**
3. **Visual Example**
4. **Exercise**
5. **Key Takeaways**

The initial camera context is a **Panasonic Lumix GH5 with a 12–42 mm lens**.

## 7. Lesson page

Route:

```text
/learn/:topicSlug/:lessonSlug
```

Recommended order:

### Lesson header

- topic label
- lesson title
- estimated time
- lesson number / total lessons

### Introduction

A short explanation of why the concept matters.

### Theory

Explain the concept in beginner-friendly language.

### Visual explanation

Use a photograph, diagram, comparison, or camera illustration where useful.

### GH5 Configuration

When relevant, show the concrete camera steps.

Example format:

```text
GH5 SETUP

1. Set the mode dial to A.
2. Rotate the appropriate control dial to change aperture.
3. Watch the f-number on screen.
4. Half-press the shutter button to meter and focus.
```

### Example

Explain what changes in the resulting image.

### Exercise

Give the learner something practical to photograph or record.

### Key takeaways

Keep this short and memorable.

### Completion/navigation

- Mark lesson complete
- Previous lesson
- Next lesson

## 11. Content-writing principles

All lessons should be written for someone starting with little or no photography knowledge.

### Use

- plain language
- short paragraphs
- concrete examples
- GH5-specific instructions when relevant
- comparisons such as “faster vs slower shutter”
- examples that explain the visual result
- exercises using ordinary subjects available at home or outside

### Avoid

- unexplained jargon
- assuming professional camera knowledge
- giant walls of text
- introducing multiple advanced concepts before the current one is understood
- telling the learner to memorize settings without explaining why
